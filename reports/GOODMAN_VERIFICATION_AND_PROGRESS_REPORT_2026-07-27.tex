{\Large\bfseries Verification and Progress on Purity of Pure\par}

{\large A machine-checked report on Goodman's project notes\par}
\vspace{8pt}
{\normalsize 27 July 2026}
\end{center}

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Purpose.}
This report records what we have verified in Jeremy Goodman's July 2026
project notes on the consistency of Purity of Pure with Bacon's logical
combinatorialism.  It has two aims.  First, it separates the claims that have
been verified exactly from claims that require qualifications, repairs, or
further formulation.  Second, it states the new theorems proved during the
formalization that genuinely narrow the open consistency problem.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line.}
Goodman's central consistency question remains open.  But the formalization
has substantially changed its shape.  The positive model program is now
reduced to constructing one enumeration of the pure unary operators.  The
enumeration must respect Bacon's substitution action, and its range must
contain every unary operator definable using the enumeration itself.  All
remaining soundness obligations for this reduction have been discharged.
Separately, a HOL--ZF model validates \PP\ and the background principles
listed below together with purity of identity, negation, constant truth,
constant falsity, the constant-function builder \(K\), conjunction, and every
curried binary truth-function.  Consequently every subcollection of
Goodman's principles whose logical-purity instances are confined to the six
displayed formulas and those sixteen truth-function formulas is consistent
in Bacon's proof system.
This does not settle the question, because Goodman's intended theory makes
every closed logical term pure at every type.
Conversely, any such model must falsify global \Ltwo\ or global \TU.  We have
also verified every determinate object-language result in the
notes, corrected a modal gap in T3, corrected a false countability argument in
M5, proved the T9 cardinal dichotomy from its explicit counting assumptions,
and isolated the exact qualifications required by several model-theoretic
claims.  Every determinate claim in the notes has now been proved, refuted and
repaired, or assigned the explicit qualification on which it is true.

\section{Formal setting and verification standard}

The formal development represents Bacon's typed language and proof system
directly in Isabelle/HOL.  It includes Bacon's system \(H\), classical and Boolean
identity principles, the Rule of Equivalence, vector Equivalence, the
definition of necessity by identity with truth, and the additional
purity/fundamentality vocabulary used in Goodman's notes.  The development
distinguishes object-language derivability from validity in a particular
model.  This matters throughout: a semantic argument valid in Bacon's
appendix model is not thereby a derivation in the theory, and an Isabelle
theorem with explicit premises does not show that those premises hold in the
intended model.

The complete audit checks the original 96 principal results together with
fifteen later \(K\)-, conjunction-, binary-truth-function, and bridge
results.  A fresh build
passes.  None of these proofs contains an admitted lemma, an untrusted proof step, or an
undischarged assumption beyond the assumptions stated in the theorem itself.
The type restriction carried by the general cardinal theorems in T9 is part of
their formally checked statement, not a further mathematical premise.

\section{Background definitions and two important distinctions}

At proposition type, Goodman's definable surrogate for fundamentality is
\[
 \funp(p)
 \quad\eqdef\quad
 \forall X\,Y\bigl((\Pure(X)\AND\Pure(Y)\AND Xp=Yp)\IC X=Y\bigr).
\]
Let \(G\) be the pure reversible unary operators, and let
\[
 X\approx Y
 \quad\eqdef\quad
 \exists Z\in G\;(X=Y\circ Z).
\]
Weak \Ltwo\ says that if \(X\) and \(Y\) are pure, \(p\) and \(q\) are
\(\funp\), and \(Xp=Yq\), then \(X\approx Y\).

Two distinctions uncovered by the formalization are central.

\paragraph{Recombination versus Exhaustion.}
Unary Recombination proves a pointwise modal identity principle.  It does not,
by itself, permit necessitating the universal closure required by the usual
\QSS\ derivation.  Zeroary Exhaustion supplies exactly that missing step.  With
unique proposition-level fundamentality, the repaired package derives the
existence of a \(\funp\) proposition.

\paragraph{Conditional model-theoretic results.}
A formally verified theorem may still have explicit premises concerning
Bacon's function domains, invariance, or \QSS.  We therefore distinguish a
verified conditional theorem from a proof that all of its premises hold in
Bacon's particular appendix model.

\section{Corrections and clarifications to the notes}

\subsection{The modal gap in T3}

The advertised argument uses necessitated \QSS\ and Persistence to move from a
possible fundamental role to an injectivity claim.  The formal derivation
reaches a statement of the form
\[
 \Diamond(Y=Z),
\]
where the desired conclusion requires \(Y=Z\).  No rule of \(H\) licenses
that transition.  The gap can be repaired in either of two principled ways.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Zeroary Exhaustion collapses pure propositions to truth and falsity,
      which supplies the required rigidity.
\item One may assume only the weaker principle that possible identity between
      pure operators implies identity.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
The unrestricted version of the second principle is false.  Thus the repair
must remain restricted to the pure case.

\subsection{The countability error in M5}

The notes propose choosing a pair \(\{s,s'\}\) outside the orbit of \(r\)
because the orbit is countable and, it is said, there are uncountably many
world-indexed pairs.  But Bacon's worlds are finite sequences of natural
numbers, hence countable.  The displayed family of pairs is therefore
countable as well.  Countability alone proves nothing.

The replacement is direct diagonalization.  Enumerate the views of \(R\) by
the worlds.  Define a proposition \(s_R\) whose truth at the singleton
\([n+2]\) disagrees with the corresponding singleton's membership in the
\(n\)-th view of \(R\), and let
\[
 s'_R=s_R\cup\{\langle\rangle\}.
\]
Membership above a nonactual world depends only on the final symbol of its
sequence.  Hence every nonactual view of \(s_R\) and \(s'_R\) is empty or
universal, while both members differ from every view of \(R\).  Exchanging
their membership in the set \(T_0\) of true propositions yields an invariant
nonidentity operator \(f_R=G_T\) with
\[
 f_R(R)=R.
\]
If \(f_R\) and identity are both included among the pure operators, evaluation at \(R\) is not
injective.  This is exactly the pre-rebuild \QSS\ obstruction the M5 strategy
needs.

\subsection{The scope of several semantic claims}

The M4 and M6 theorems are genuine mathematical results, but at present they
are conditional.  They assume that every operator counted as pure belongs to
Bacon's function domain and is invariant, together with necessitated \QSS\
and, for some results, the presence of identity and the two constant
operators.
The proofs do not yet instantiate these assumptions for Bacon's complete
stock of operators denoted by closed terms containing only logical
vocabulary.  We therefore do not count them as unconditional
verifications of the corresponding claims about Bacon's particular glued
model.

\section{New theorems advancing the consistency question}

The audit was not merely defensive.  The formalization has produced several
theorems not stated in the original notes.  These results materially narrow
both the positive and negative programs.

\subsection{Recombination, Exhaustion, and the existence of a
\texorpdfstring{\(\funp\)}{fun-prime} proposition}

\begin{theorem}
Unary Recombination yields the pointwise \QSS\ core.  Adding zeroary
Exhaustion yields the necessitated universal form needed for \QSS.  Together
with unique proposition-level fundamentality, the resulting stock proves
that a \(\funp\) proposition exists.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
This theorem removes the previously assumed existential \(\funp\) premise
from the premises used in T6.  It also makes explicit exactly where Exhaustion,
rather than Recombination, enters the object-language argument.

\subsection{An exact semantic consequence of T6}

\begin{theorem}
Any model validating Bacon's proof theory, Recombination, the
proposition-type instance of Exhaustion, unique proposition-level
fundamentality, and \PP\ must falsify global \Ltwo\ or global \TU:
\[
 \NOT\,\mathsf{GValid}(\Ltwo)
 \quad\OR\quad
 \NOT\,\mathsf{GValid}(\TU).
\]
Moreover, some world in the constructed model falsifies one of the
two closed formulas.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
Here \(\mathsf{GValid}(A)\) means that the closed formula \(A\) is true at
every world of the model.
This formalizes the central dilemma in Goodman's notes.  It does not select a
disjunct.  A positive model may exploit either cross-kind collisions on
\(\funp\) inputs or a pure reversible operator that is neither uniformly
truth-preserving nor uniformly truth-flipping.

\subsection{Adding purity of constant truth and constant falsity}

Let \(C_{\top}\) and \(C_{\bot}\) be the unary operators
\[
 C_{\top}(p)=\mathsf{truth}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 C_{\bot}(p)=\mathsf{falsity}.
\]
We now make these two closed logical operators pure, in addition to identity
and negation.  Closure of purity under application gives truth and falsity at
type \(t\), the four unary operators at type \(t\IC t\), and the proposition
classifying those four unary operators at type \((t\IC t)\IC t\).

\begin{theorem}[The four-logical-operators fragment]
There is a set-theoretic HOL--ZF tree model with Henkin domains that validates
all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item purity of identity, negation, \(C_{\top}\), and \(C_{\bot}\);
\item \PP;
\item every instance of closure of purity under application;
\item zeroary and unary Recombination and Exhaustion;
\item Modalized Functionality at arbitrary types; and
\item unique fundamentality at proposition type and the absence of
      fundamental entities at every other type.
\end{enumerate}
The collection consisting of exactly these principles is a subcollection of
Goodman's principles and is consistent in Bacon's proof system.
\end{theorem}

Let \(T_G\) be Goodman's full collection of principles, and let
\(\mathsf{LP}\subseteq T_G\) be its logical-purity schema.  Put
\[
 \mathsf{LP}_{4}=
 \{\,\Pure(I),\Pure(N),\Pure(C_{\top}),\Pure(C_{\bot})\,\},
\]
where \(I\) is identity and \(N\) is negation.  The bridge theorem establishes,
for every \(U\),
\[
 U\subseteq T_G
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 U\cap\mathsf{LP}\subseteq\mathsf{LP}_{4}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathsf{Con}_{\mathrm{CEV}^{+}}(U).
\tag{IC}
\]
No finiteness assumption on \(U\) is required.  This does not settle
Goodman's question, because the logical-purity schema in the intended theory
includes every closed logical term, including terms at higher types.  The
following subsection strengthens this result by adding purity of \(K\).  The
subsection after it adds conjunction.  A complete result requires all closed
logical terms at all types.

\subsection{Adding purity of \texorpdfstring{\(K\)}{K}}

Let
\[
 K=\lambda p{:}t.\lambda q{:}t.\,p.
\]
Thus \(Kp\) is the constant unary operator with value \(p\).  In the preceding
model, the pure propositions lie in the truth and falsity classes.  Hence
closure of purity under application sends \(K\) applied to a pure proposition
to one of the two constant unary classes already present in the four-operator
stock.

\begin{theorem}[The constant-function-builder fragment]
There is a set-theoretic HOL--ZF tree model with Henkin domains that validates
all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item purity of identity, negation, \(C_{\top}\), \(C_{\bot}\), and \(K\);
\item \PP;
\item every instance of closure of purity under application;
\item the zeroary and unary instances of Recombination and Exhaustion;
\item Modalized Functionality at arbitrary types; and
\item unique fundamentality at proposition type and the absence of
      fundamental entities at every other type.
\end{enumerate}
The collection consisting of exactly these principles is a subcollection of
Goodman's principles and is consistent in Bacon's proof system.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The qualification in item 4 is important: this model verifies only the
zeroary and unary instances in the present \(\mathsf{QLN}\) stock.  It does
not establish a binary instance or the unrestricted \(\mathsf{QLN}\) schema.

Let
\[
 \mathsf{LP}_{K}=
 \mathsf{LP}_{4}\cup\{\,\Pure(K)\,\}.
\]
The strengthened bridge theorem establishes, for every \(U\),
\[
 U\subseteq T_G
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 U\cap\mathsf{LP}\subseteq\mathsf{LP}_{K}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathsf{Con}_{\mathrm{CEV}^{+}}(U).
\tag{KIC}
\]
As in \((\mathrm{IC})\), no finiteness assumption on \(U\) is required.  This
is a genuine extension of the four-operator result: the higher-type closed
logical operator \(K:t\IC(t\IC t)\) is now itself pure, and its applications
remain inside the verified pure stock.  Goodman's full question nevertheless
remains open, since the intended logical-purity schema contains every closed
logical term at every type.  The following subsection strengthens the result
by adding conjunction.

\subsection{Adding purity of conjunction}

Let
\[
 \mathsf{And}=\lambda p{:}t.\lambda q{:}t.\,(p\wedge q).
\]
The preceding model has exactly the truth and falsity classes as pure
propositions.  Consequently
\[
 \mathsf{And}\,\top\approx I,
 \qquad
 \mathsf{And}\,\bot\approx C_{\bot}.
\]
Both unary classes already occur in the verified pure stock, so closure of
purity under application introduces no new unary operator.

\begin{theorem}[The conjunction fragment]
There is a set-theoretic HOL--ZF tree model with Henkin domains that validates
all of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item purity of identity, negation, \(C_{\top}\), \(C_{\bot}\), \(K\), and
      \(\mathsf{And}\);
\item \PP;
\item every instance of closure of purity under application;
\item the zeroary and unary instances of Recombination and Exhaustion;
\item Modalized Functionality at arbitrary types; and
\item unique fundamentality at proposition type and the absence of
      fundamental entities at every other type.
\end{enumerate}
The collection consisting of exactly these principles is a subcollection of
Goodman's principles and is consistent in Bacon's proof system.
\end{theorem}

Let
\[
 \mathsf{LP}_{\wedge}
 =\mathsf{LP}_{K}\cup\{\,\Pure(\mathsf{And})\,\}.
\]
The bridge theorem establishes, for every \(U\),
\[
 U\subseteq T_G
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 U\cap\mathsf{LP}\subseteq\mathsf{LP}_{\wedge}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathsf{Con}_{\mathrm{CEV}^{+}}(U).
\tag{AIC}
\]
No finiteness assumption on \(U\) is required.  As before, the verified QLN
stock is zeroary and unary; no binary or unrestricted QLN claim is made.
This remains a fragment theorem, since Goodman's logical-purity schema
contains every higher-order closed logical term.

\subsection{Adding all binary truth-functions}

For each Boolean function
\[
 F:\{\mathsf{true},\mathsf{false}\}^{2}

\subsection{Reduction of the positive program to one equation}

We constructed a set-theoretic formalization of Bacon's substitution model and
verified directly:

\begin{itemize}
\item every axiom of Bacon's base proof theory;
\item the vector Equivalence rule;
\item beta and eta compatibility;
\item the Boolean, quantifier, identity, and modal clauses used by the
      central theory;
\item the proposition-type instance of Exhaustion for every relevant choice
      of pure entities.
\end{itemize}

Let \(E\) be an enumerator of unary operators, represented in the language by
a term of type \(\mathsf{Ind}\IC(t\IC t)\).  It must commute with taking the
view from any substitution, as Bacon's function-domain condition requires.
Let \(\mathcal C_E\) be the class of unary operators denoted by closed
expressions built from the logical vocabulary, the natural-number indices,
\(E\), and application.

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient condition for a model]
If \(E\) has the preceding properties and satisfies
\[
 \mathcal C_E=\{E(n):n\in\N\},
 \tag{SE}
\]
then the resulting model validates Bacon's base theory, Recombination, the
proposition-type instance of Exhaustion, unique proposition-level
fundamentality, and \PP\ at the target type.  Hence \((\mathrm{SE})\) gives an
affirmative answer to Goodman's question.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Equivalent formulation]
The inclusion
\[
 \{E(n):n\in\N\}\subseteq\mathcal C_E
\]
is automatic.  Thus \((\mathrm{SE})\) is equivalent to the assertion that
every well-typed unary expression built in the indicated way has the same
denotation as \(E(n)\) for some \(n\).
\end{theorem}

\noindent
This is the cleanest current formulation of the positive problem.  No
independent soundness premise remains.  The residual task is not to construct
an arbitrary set-theoretic enumeration, but an enumeration compatible with
Bacon's substitution action whose range contains every operator definable
using the enumeration itself.

\subsection{Diagonal restrictions on any solution}

\begin{theorem}[Restriction on definable diagonal predicates]
If \(E\) satisfies \((\mathrm{SE})\), then there is no expression
\(P:\mathsf{Ind}\IC t\), built in the indicated way, that diagonalizes against
the enumeration at the actual world:
\[
 P(n)\quad\IFF\quad \NOT\,\mathsf{Truth}(E(n)(P))
\]
in the required sense.  In particular, no definable predicate can assign
different truth values to all indices in the naive Cantorian way.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
This explains why a straightforward internal enumeration diagonal does not
immediately refute \((\mathrm{SE})\): compatibility with Bacon's substitution
action collapses the obvious index predicates.

We also formalized a more flexible diagonal \(D_R(E,H,R)\).  It treats two
representations as equivalent according to a relation \(R\), rather than
requiring literal identity.  The construction is a closed term containing
only logical vocabulary; consequently it is pure whenever its parameters
\(H\) and \(R\) are pure.

\begin{theorem}[Generalized diagonal constraint]
Assume \((\mathrm{SE})\).  Suppose \(R\) cannot distinguish propositions that
agree in truth value at the actual world, and \(H\) distinguishes the relevant
indices.  Then some proposition \(H(q)\) has two representations that are not
\(R\)-related.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
One natural choice says that \(F\) and \(G\) are related at \(q\) when
\[
 F=G\circ\psi
 \quad\text{for some bijection \(\psi\) with}\quad \psi(q)=q.
\]
This relation is itself expressible using only logical vocabulary and cannot
distinguish propositions agreeing in actual truth value.  It relates every
pair obtained by a reversible change of input that fixes \(q\), eliminating
that entire class of prospective counterexamples.  What remains is either to
show that it relates all representations of some suitable \(H(q)\), or to
construct two representations that it does not relate.

\subsection{A uniform M5 obstruction}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform version of the M5 construction]
For every candidate proposition \(R\), there is a pair
\(\{s_R,s'_R\}\) disjoint from the set of all views of \(R\).  Every
nonactual view of either member is empty or universal.  The associated invariant
transposition fixes \(R\) and is not identity.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
This is stronger and cleaner than the original M5 selection argument.  It
shows that an exotic invariant operator fixing any preassigned \(R\) can be
constructed without cardinal assumptions.  It also sharpens the role of
Bacon's rebuilding theorem: the hard part is not constructing the exotic
operator, but rebuilding a generic fundamental proposition relative to the
enlarged definable stock.

\subsection{The T9 and M7 reductions}

\begin{theorem}[T9 dichotomy]
If \PC\ supplies an injection \(\Pow(K)\to P\) into the pure unary operators,
and every \(\approx\)-class injects into \(G\), then
\[
 K\text{ is finite}
 \quad\OR\quad
 |G|\geq 2^{|K|}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Invariant reachability]
Every proposition is reachable from \(r\) by an invariant operator if and
only if
\[
 i\longmapsto i\cdot r
\]
is injective.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The second theorem turns the open invariant form of Fundamental Completeness
into a precise property of the chosen fundamental proposition.  It also
confirms that the issue depends on Bacon's particular construction rather
than following from general facts about substitutions.

\section{Where the open question now stands}

The current affirmative and negative routes can be stated compactly.

\paragraph{Affirmative route.}
Construct an \(E\) satisfying \((\mathrm{SE})\): \(E\) must respect Bacon's
substitution action, and every pure unary operator definable using \(E\) must
already occur among the values \(E(n)\).  We have explored systematic ways of
closing a countable collection of operators while preserving a proposition
that distinguishes its members.  Those proposals remain preliminary and are
not counted among the verified results in this report.

\paragraph{Negative route.}
Show that every proposed enumeration omits some pure unary operator definable
from that very enumeration.  The generalized diagonal \(D_R\) identifies one
precise route: find a pure relation \(R\) that respects actual truth and
relates all representations of a suitable proposition \(H(q)\).  The natural
relation generated by bijections fixing \(q\) handles representations related
by reversible changes of input, but not yet all representations.

\paragraph{Relative plausibility.}
The formal work has not settled which answer is more likely.  It has, however,
made an affirmative answer significantly more concrete: it is enough to
construct one enumeration satisfying \((\mathrm{SE})\) in the verified
substitution model.  At the same time, the diagonal restrictions show why the
most direct Cantorian objection to such an enumeration fails.  A separate
positive result now handles every subcollection of Goodman's principles whose
logical-purity instances are confined to identity, negation, constant truth,
constant falsity, \(K\), conjunction, and the sixteen curried binary
truth-functions.  The next extensions add necessity and possibility
separately.

\section{Recommended next steps}

We recommend preserving the following lines of attack.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Continue adding logical purity.}  Identity, negation, constant
      truth, constant falsity, \(K\), conjunction, and every curried binary
      truth-function are now handled.  Add necessity and possibility in
      separate stages while preserving closure of purity under application,
      the verified zeroary and unary instances of Recombination and
      Exhaustion, Modalized Functionality, and \PP.  If a stage fails,
      distinguish failure of the particular tree model from a derivation of
      contradiction from Goodman's principles.  A complete result requires
      every closed logical term at every type.
\item \textbf{Positive construction.}  Test whether a countable collection of
      pure operators can preserve a proposition separating its members while
      remaining closed under every operation definable in the language.
\item \textbf{Sharp negative test.}  Search for one closed logical unary
      \(F\) whose action makes two distinct members of the proposed pure
      collection agree at its separating proposition.  Such an \(F\) would
      refute that proposed construction immediately.
\item \textbf{Finish the source audit only when formulas are available.}
      Obtain an exact T7b statement, an exact formulation of the wide-Fun
      discussion, and an exact statement of the proposed general collision
      argument before encoding them.
\item \textbf{Keep model claims and proof-theoretic claims separate.}
      In particular, do not promote the conditional M4/M6 stock theorems to
      claims about Bacon's exact model until their stock premises are
      instantiated.
\end{enumerate}
